\section{Introduction}
The Sparse Matrix-Vector Multiplication (SpMV) operation, defined as $y = Ax$, costitutes a critical computational kernel for High-Performance Computing (HPC) and scientific simulations.
Due to its low arithmetic intensity, quantifiable as $2 \times nnz$ total operations, SpMV is an inherently memory-bound operation.
Its effiency is further hindered by the irregulare nature of memory accesses, which are strictly dependent on the matrix sparsity structure.
This study analyzes the impact of storage formats and parallelization stategies on the NVIDIA Ampere architecture, comparing custom CUDA implementations (COO, CSR, and CSR-Vector) with the highly optimized cuSPARSE library.
The investigation evaluates performance through key metrics: kernel execution time, throughput (GFLOPS), and effective Bandwidth (BW).
Beyond a standard comparative evaluation, this work examines the link between data topology and performance, analyzing how parallel computing efficiency is influenced by memory organization and cache hit/miss rates.
The analysis demonstrates how the structural characteristics of matrices from different application domains-raning from fluid dynamics to socail network graphs-decisively influence the overall operation speed and hardware utilization.
The objective is to provide a comprehensive experimetnal study that clarifies under which structural conditions specific formats or kernels should be prederred to mitigate the instrinsic bottlenecks of sparse computation.

\subsection{Input Generation and Precision}
The test utilize single precision (\texttt{Float32}), whic is the standard for high performance computing on GPUs. The dense vector $x$ ($N \times 1$) is generated using the \texttt{fill\_random\_vector} function, which initializes the pseudo-random generator with a fixed seed (\texttt{srand(42)}).
Values are normalized within the range $[0, 1]$ using the ratio \texttt{rand()/RAND\_MAX}.
This approach ensures the reproducibility od results between CPU and GPU, isolating the perfomance analysis solely to the strucutral characteristics of the matrices and the efficiency of the kernels.
